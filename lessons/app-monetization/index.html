Publishing an app and earning from it are different jobs. You need a way for the right people to discover the product, understand why it matters, pay through a method they can use, and receive the access they bought.

01 — THE COMPLETE PATH

Own the journey from discovery to payment.

Discovery→Trust→Payment→Access
01Distribution

Where customers find the product: useful pages, demos, direct outreach, communities, referrals, or partnerships.

02Payment

How money reaches you: choose based on buyer location, eligibility, fees, payout support, and the billing experience.

03Delivery

Where the product runs: a mobile-friendly web app or PWA with clear access and support.

04Trust

Why customers feel safe buying: a clear outcome, real proof, transparent pricing, and a route to help.

!
Do not confuse the three jobs

A payment provider does not find customers for you. Hosting does not create demand. Distribution, payment, and paid access are connected, but each needs to work.

02 — PRICING MODELS

Choose a model that fits the value.

MODEL 01 · SUBSCRIPTION

Charge for ongoing value.

Customers pay monthly or yearly for continued access, updates, or a workflow they rely on repeatedly. Good fit: SaaS and recurring professional work.

MODEL 02 · PAY-PER-USE

Charge per task or unit.

Customers buy credits or pay for each generation, export, or task. Good fit: tools with variable usage or operating costs.

MODEL 03 · FREEMIUM

Let users experience value first.

A useful free tier can build adoption; charge for higher limits, advanced features, or team workflows. The free experience should reveal value, not hide the product's purpose.

MODEL 04 · LIFETIME LICENCE

One payment for a defined licence.

State exactly what the buyer receives, including support and update terms. Good fit: focused utilities with predictable long-term costs.

MODEL 05 · BUSINESS LICENCE

Sell to a team or organisation.

Price by seat, team, usage, or negotiated agreement. Good fit: software that solves a costly workflow problem for a business.

Models can be combined, but begin with the simplest one that matches how customers receive value. More pricing options do not automatically mean more revenue.

03 — PAYMENTS AND DELIVERY

Make it easy to buy — and reliable to access.

₦Nigerian customers

Investigate providers such as Paystack for local payments. Check supported methods, fees, settlement, recurring billing, and your eligibility before choosing.

International customers

Compare merchant-of-record or digital-sales providers against current Nigerian seller eligibility, supported products, fees, and payout availability. Do not assume a provider supports every country.

Host a responsive web app, connect a domain, and consider PWA installation where browser support allows. An app store can be useful, but it is not the only way to deliver software.

✓
Protect paid access

Verify payment on the server using the provider's verification flow and webhooks. Do not unlock paid features solely because a browser reaches a success page. Make refunds, renewals, cancellation, and support terms understandable.

04 — FIND CUSTOMERS

Do not wait for discovery to happen.

  • Direct outreach: identify people with the problem, show a working demo, and ask about a paid pilot.
  • Communities: contribute useful answers before pitching in founder, professional, or customer communities.
  • Search content: publish pages that answer specific questions your intended customers already ask.
  • Partnerships and referrals: find people or organisations already trusted by your target audience.
  • Launch platforms: use directories or launch communities when you are ready to collect feedback and follow up.

For Nigerian customers, test relevant WhatsApp referrals, professional networks, partnerships, and direct sales. For international customers, combine useful content, niche communities, and targeted outreach. Choose a channel based on where your customers already pay attention, not because it is fashionable.

05 — THE MINIMUM STACK

Enough tools to ship. Not a tool museum.

  • Hosting: Vercel or Netlify.
  • Code and collaboration: GitHub.
  • Database and authentication: choose a suitable setup; Supabase and Neon are alternatives for many database needs, and you usually do not need both.
  • Payments: choose a provider based on buyer geography and eligibility.
  • Reliability: error monitoring such as Sentry when the product needs it.
  • Analytics: measure activation, feature use, checkout starts, conversion, and retention.
  • Support: give customers a clear way to get help.

Add tools when they solve a real operational problem. Do not mistake a larger stack for a more sellable product.

06 — THE FIRST 30 DAYS

One product. One complete path.

W1Make it sellable

Choose one product, define the paid outcome, set up checkout, and verify the full payment-to-access flow.

W2Talk to likely buyers

Reach out to people who may have the problem. Learn whether they will pay, not only whether they like the idea.

W3Test distribution

Publish a demo, share useful content, and test one focused community or outreach channel.

W4Fix the bottleneck

Review visits, activation, purchases, repeat usage, and feedback. Improve the weakest step before adding complexity.

YOUR CUSTOMER JOURNEY

My customer is a specific buyer. They will discover me through a focused channel. They pay using a supported payment method. After payment, they receive a clear, verifiable outcome.

YOUR NEXT STEP

Start with the customer, then build the route.

Choose one audience, one pricing model, and one distribution channel. Test the whole journey before adding more payment providers or infrastructure.
