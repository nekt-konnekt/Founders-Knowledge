You've built your app, deployed the frontend, connected a database, and shared the link. Everything works. Then you return after a quiet period and discover that a database-backed feature no longer responds. This is not just an infrastructure issue; it can become a product issue.

01 — THE ASSUMPTION

A free service isn't automatically an always-available service.

When you're building on a tight budget, a free backend can feel like a permanent resource. You create a project, connect your application, and assume it will remain available whenever someone visits.

But free services can have inactivity policies, usage limits, or compute that sleeps when idle. These behaviours are different, and understanding the difference matters.

!
The product lesson

Infrastructure limits can become user-experience problems, even when your application code hasn't changed.

02 — WHY PROJECTS PAUSE

Supabase Free projects can pause after inactivity.

Supabase's Free Plan has an inactivity-based project pausing policy. This can affect prototypes, experiments, and products that receive little or no qualifying activity for a while. The exact policy can change, so check the current Supabase project pausing guide rather than relying on a remembered number of days.

A paused project may need to be restored before its database-backed features work again. Read the provider's current recovery rules and understand what is retained and for how long.

Deploying a frontend to Vercel does not, by itself, keep a separate Supabase database active. The two services have separate infrastructure and usage policies.

03 — KNOW YOUR DEPENDENCIES

Your application is a collection of services.

A typical web app relies on several components, often hosted by different providers.

01Frontend

The interface users see, possibly hosted on Vercel.

02Backend and database

Server-side logic and the data your features depend on.

03External services

Authentication, email, file storage, analytics, and integrations.

04Recovery path

How you detect a failure, restore service, and protect data.

Each component can have its own limits and availability behaviour. A live website does not prove every dependency is ready to serve a request.

04 — COMPARE THE BEHAVIOUR

Pausing and scaling to zero are not the same thing.

Automatic project pausing
A provider suspends a project under its inactivity policy; you may need to restore it.

Scale to zero
Compute resources can suspend when idle and resume when requests arrive, potentially adding a cold-start delay.

Always-on compute
Resources remain allocated, subject to the provider's service terms, limits, and outages.

Neon supports scale-to-zero compute, which is different from Supabase's project pausing policy. That difference alone does not make one provider universally better. Compare current plans, connection behaviour, recovery options, backups, and cost for your workload. Check the Supabase pricing page and Neon pricing page for current details.

?
Don't game the inactivity rule

Artificial pings may not count as qualifying activity and aren't a substitute for an availability plan that matches your users' expectations.

PUT IT TO WORK

Before you choose a backend, check these six things.

  1. What happens when the project receives no traffic for several days?
  2. Does compute sleep, or can the whole project be paused?
  3. What will users experience when the first request arrives after inactivity?
  4. How do you restore the service, and what data is retained?
  5. What backups and recovery options are included in your plan?
  6. What does the availability you need actually cost?
! Reality check

A free tier can be suitable for a production app if its limits and availability behaviour fit the product. A paid plan isn't a guarantee against every outage either. Match the service to the reliability your users need.

THE TAKEAWAY

Your infrastructure decision is also a product decision. Choose with availability, recovery, and real costs in mind—not just the size of the free tier.
