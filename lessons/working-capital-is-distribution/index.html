Working capital is distribution.

In physical B2B markets, a sale is not complete when a customer signs. It is complete when the service is installed, working and paid for.

“

A startup can win a contract and still fail to deliver it. The buyer is ready. The product works. The project team is waiting. But the batteries, panels, devices or other critical components are not in the country yet — and the company cannot afford to buy them before the customer pays. The sales pipeline looks healthy while the delivery engine is cash-starved.

01 — WHAT BII IS FINANCING

The $7 million is for the gap between order and installation.

On 29 October 2026, British International Investment (BII), the UK's development finance institution, announced a $7 million debt facility for Okra Solar, an Australian-founded provider of mesh-grid technology. The facility is intended to let Okra procure solar panels, batteries and other long-lead components in advance and in larger volumes, rather than waiting for each project transaction to close.

The announced target is equipment for approximately 150,000 new connections, with the potential to reach 750,000 people in rural Nigeria. Okra's reported installed base is more than 13,000 households and 2.3 megawatts of capacity. The company says advance procurement should reduce equipment delivery time to Nigeria from about four months to about two.

ANNOUNCED VS. ACHIEVED

  • Announced: a $7 million debt facility for working capital and advance procurement.
  • Target: equipment for roughly 150,000 new connections, potentially reaching 750,000 people.
  • Existing base: more than 13,000 households and 2.3MW of installed capacity, as reported in coverage of the deal.
  • Not yet an outcome: the 150,000 connections, the 750,000 people reached and the shorter delivery time are targets or expected effects, not completed results.

The facility is debt, not a grant and not an equity round. It is designed to finance a specific operating constraint: buying inventory early enough to deliver when a project developer is ready. The available announcement does not disclose the facility's interest rate, tenor, collateral, covenants or drawdown conditions. Those terms matter to the economics and should not be guessed.

Sources: Launch Base Africa's deal analysis and the BII-sourced announcement reproduced by Renewables Rising.

02 — WHY PROCUREMENT IS DISTRIBUTION

A buyer cannot use a product that has not arrived.

Okra's mesh-grid systems connect neighbouring homes and businesses through distributed solar generation, batteries and smart controls. The company sells technology to energy developers who use it to serve communities. That is a B2B delivery chain, not a simple app download.

For this kind of business, distribution includes much more than finding a buyer. The company has to forecast demand, secure components, pay suppliers, ship and clear equipment, hold stock, install systems, commission them, support local operators and collect the money due under the project contract.

If suppliers require payment months before the customer pays, the company is financing the delivery gap. If it waits until every contract is signed before ordering parts, projects wait in a queue. If it buys too much stock too early, cash sits in inventory that may be delayed, damaged, mismatched to a project or made more expensive by currency movements.

!
Sales pipeline is not working capital

A lead is not a purchase order. A purchase order is not a deposit. A deposit is not cash collected. Treat each stage differently when deciding how much inventory to buy and how much debt the business can safely carry.

This is why working capital can behave like distribution infrastructure. It does not create customer demand by itself. It makes it possible to fulfil demand at the time and place the customer needs the service.

03 — WHAT THE MESH-GRID EVIDENCE SAYS

The technology has a use case, not a universal advantage.

There is independent Nigerian evidence worth reading before treating mesh-grids as a blanket replacement for mini-grids. In a 2025 study, CrossBoundary's Innovation Lab tested two configurations: a full mesh-grid installation and a mesh-grid extension to an existing mini-grid.

THE RESULTS WERE MIXED — AND THAT IS USEFUL

  • Full installation: infrastructure cost was $803 per connection, compared with $1,358 at the mini-grid control site — a 41% reduction.
  • Extension to an existing mini-grid: mesh-grid extensions were about 45% more expensive than standard mini-grid extensions, because the existing site already had generation and storage capacity.
  • Revenue: full mesh-grid installations recorded 48% lower revenue than the mini-grid control sites; extension mesh-grids recorded 25% higher average revenue per user despite lower consumption.

The lesson is not that mesh-grids always win. It is that site conditions, existing infrastructure, customer density, expected energy use and the revenue model determine whether the architecture makes sense. CrossBoundary also said the ability to serve higher-load business uses and long-term durability still needed further testing.

For founders, this is a better way to evaluate a technical claim: ask where it works, where it does not, what the comparison is, and which costs or revenues the study measured. A technology advantage at one site is not a universal unit-economics advantage.

Source: CrossBoundary Innovation Lab, 10 June 2025.

04 — MAP THE CASH-TO-CUSTOMER CYCLE

Find the number of days your business must finance.

Before taking debt or buying inventory in bulk, draw the path from a customer's need to cash received. Put a date, an owner and a cash amount beside every step.

  • Demand: is there a signed purchase order, a deposit, a framework agreement or only a sales conversation?
  • Procurement: when must you pay the supplier, in what currency, and how long until the goods are ready?
  • Import and storage: include freight, customs, clearing, inland transport, insurance, warehousing and damage risk.
  • Delivery: who installs and commissions the product, and what must be true before the customer accepts it?
  • Collection: does the customer pay on delivery, after acceptance, by milestone, or after a grant or project verification process?
  • After-sales: what stock, technicians and cash must remain available for maintenance and warranty claims?

The cash-conversion gap is the time between paying for the inputs and receiving the related customer or project cash. A simple first estimate is:

A STARTING ESTIMATE — NOT A FULL CASH-FLOW MODEL

Inventory cash required ≈ monthly landed component cost × procurement lead-time in months, plus a justified safety-stock and import buffer.

If your deployment rate is ₦20 million of landed components per month and the reliable procurement lead time is three months, the inventory alone may tie up roughly ₦60 million before safety stock, installation labour, financing costs or customer-payment delays. Use your own actual costs and dates; this example is illustrative.

Run the calculation by customer cohort or project, not only at company level. One customer may pay a 50% deposit while another pays 60 days after commissioning. Averaging them together can hide the project that consumes all your cash.

05 — MATCH FINANCE TO THE CYCLE

Borrow against a real operating rhythm, not an optimistic pipeline.

Different financing instruments solve different gaps. They are not interchangeable.

  • Customer deposits and milestone billing reduce the amount you must fund yourself when buyers can reasonably pay before delivery.
  • Supplier credit moves some of the cash burden to the supplier, but usually requires a track record, reliable orders or a strong relationship.
  • Purchase-order or inventory finance can fund confirmed orders and identifiable goods. It is a poor fit for speculative stock with no clear buyer or exit route.
  • Asset or project finance may fit installed assets that generate predictable cash over time, provided repayment dates match the cash they produce.
  • Equity is more appropriate when demand, technology or the business model is still uncertain and the company cannot responsibly promise a fixed repayment schedule.

Debt can accelerate a proven cycle, but it can also magnify a mismatch. If customers pay late, the naira weakens, equipment is delayed or installation capacity is insufficient, the loan repayment date does not automatically move with the project.

For an early-stage company, the first objective is not to build a giant warehouse. It is to prove one repeatable route from order to installation to cash collection. Buy more inventory only when demand quality, deployment capacity and the repayment path justify it.

06 — A 30-DAY FOUNDER TEST

Test the delivery economics before you scale the stock.

WEEK BY WEEK

  • Week 1 — Map the cycle. List every step from signed order to cash received. Record lead times, deposits, supplier terms, installation time, acceptance criteria and receivable days.
  • Week 2 — Separate real demand from pipeline. Mark each opportunity as signed and funded, signed but unfunded, or still a sales prospect. Get current supplier quotes and confirm which components actually constrain delivery.
  • Week 3 — Model three cases. Build conservative, base and stretch cash forecasts. Include foreign exchange, freight, customs, storage, damage, warranty, financing costs and late customer payments.
  • Week 4 — Run one controlled batch. Track order-to-installation time, on-time fulfilment, stockouts, cost per commissioned unit, project gross contribution, cash-conversion days and overdue receivables.

Do not judge the pilot by the number of units ordered. Judge it by the number installed, accepted, paid for and still working — at a contribution margin that can support the next batch.

For a product manager, this also changes the product roadmap. Standardise components where possible, design for local repair, make commissioning repeatable, expose inventory and service status, and build monitoring that helps a small field team support more sites. A feature that reduces installation time or repeat visits may be worth more than another dashboard screen.

THE TAKEAWAY

In physical B2B markets, distribution is not just finding buyers. It is having the cash, inventory, installation capacity and service system to deliver when buyers are ready.

Evidence & limits

Reported deal facts: BII's $7 million debt facility, the intended 150,000 connections, the potential 750,000 people reached, Okra's existing installed base and the expected procurement-time reduction are reported in the BII-sourced announcement and independent coverage. They are not evidence that the target connections have already been delivered.

Independent technical evidence: CrossBoundary's 2025 Innovation Lab study compared two mesh-grid configurations in Nigeria and reported both cost advantages and cases where mesh-grid extensions were more expensive. The study itself says more work is needed on high-load productive uses and long-term durability.

Not disclosed in the materials reviewed: the BII facility's interest rate, tenor, collateral, covenants and drawdown conditions; the first delivery milestones under the facility; and whether the 150,000-connection target will be achieved. This article is an operating lesson, not a recommendation to borrow or invest.
