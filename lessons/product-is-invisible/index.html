Do not confuse attention with a repeatable way to reach the right people.

Publishing a product is not the same as making it discoverable. If strangers cannot find it, understand its value, or return after the first visit, building more features may not fix the real bottleneck.

01 — A FIELD NOTE, NOT A BENCHMARK

One founder's snapshot.

~600K organic views→~1.7K downloads→31 paid subscribers

A founder reported these results for a health app over about three weeks, attributing most of the attention to organic content rather than app-store search. The same founder said two earlier apps built around store optimisation earned very little.

!
Interpret this carefully

These figures are self-reported, unverified, and from one story. They are not a controlled experiment, a reliable conversion rate, an industry average, or a target to copy. The founder also said much of the attention came from one small market.

The useful question is not “How do I get 600,000 views?” It is “What repeatable path helps the right people discover, try, and keep using this product?”

02 — FIND THE BOTTLENECK

Which symptom looks most like yours?

SYMPTOM 01 · CRICKETS

Nobody finds you.

There is no dependable discovery channel. Start by choosing one or two channels and working them consistently long enough to learn.

  • Make your key pages clear to people and search systems.
  • Contribute where the intended customers already gather.
  • Track qualified visits, not just impressions.

SYMPTOM 02 · VIRAL, THEN DEAD

One spike, then silence.

Attention came, but little of it became an audience you can reach again.

  • Offer a clear, consent-based way to stay in touch.
  • Turn useful high-performing content into durable pages.
  • Learn what the spike's audience actually needed.

SYMPTOM 03 · TRAFFIC, NO SALES

People visit but do not buy.

The page, promise, audience, proof, or timing may not fit.

  • Align the headline with the message that brought people there.
  • Make one next action clear.
  • Show credible proof before asking for commitment.

SYMPTOM 04 · SIGN UP, GHOST

Users try it once and vanish.

The first experience may not lead to a recurring outcome or habit.

  • Find what returning users do differently.
  • Make the first useful outcome easier to reach.
  • Give users a relevant reason to return.

03 — THREE SYSTEMS TO TEST

Use frameworks as experiments, not promises.

01Answer-oriented content

Write clear pages that directly answer real questions. Good structure can help human readers and conventional search. Treat claims about AI answer engines and llms.txt cautiously: there is no guaranteed ranking shortcut.

02Distribution as a product feature

Look for a natural moment when users can share a useful result, invite someone, or show their work. Make sharing relevant and respectful rather than forcing virality.

03Human-led AI

Use AI to accelerate drafts and analysis, but keep people responsible for judgment, accuracy, outreach, relationships, and strategy.

04Focused channel work

Pick a channel that fits the product and the audience. Contribute consistently, follow up, and measure the journey from discovery to useful activation.

✳

04 — MAKE THE NEXT WEEK USEFUL

A simple distribution sprint.

  • Define the audience: name a specific group with a specific problem.
  • Choose one channel: select where those people already spend attention.
  • Make one useful contribution: publish an answer, show a demo, or help someone solve a real problem.
  • Give a clear next step: let interested people try the product or voluntarily subscribe for more useful information.
  • Review behavior: track qualified visits, activation, return usage, and sales where relevant.
WRITE YOUR EXPERIMENT

For a specific audience, I will use one channel to offer one useful message or experience. I will review one meaningful outcome after a defined period.

05 — KEEP THE CLAIMS HONEST

Distribution matters. It is not the only reason products fail.

A weak problem, unclear value, poor experience, or lack of retention can also stop a product from growing. More traffic will not repair every product problem. Diagnose the actual constraint before increasing promotion.

This report was adapted from public founder discussions. Results were self-reported and not independently verified; ideas are presented as prompts for testing, not universal advice.

YOUR NEXT STEP

Find the people before adding more features.

Write down who the product is for, where those people already look for help, and the smallest useful experiment you can run this week.
